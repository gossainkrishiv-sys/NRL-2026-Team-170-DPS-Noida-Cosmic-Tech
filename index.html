Our flagship engineering project · IPRV V3.1

INDIAN POTHOLE REPAIR VEHICLE.

An integrated robotic concept for detecting road damage, recording its location, navigating to it and carrying out a targeted repair.

ENGINEERING • ROBOTICS • AUTOMATION

From road damage to a repair-ready target.

IPRV (Indian Pothole Repair Vehicle) is a four-wheel robotic prototype designed to connect pothole inspection with repair. Instead of treating detection as the whole task, the system is designed to gather information about a damaged road area, estimate its geometry, record where it is and use that information to guide the vehicle and its repair mechanism.

The goal is to make road-condition information more useful for planned maintenance and, where practical, reduce the need for workers to inspect damaged areas directly beside moving traffic.

02 / How the system works

ONE CONNECTED WORKFLOW.

01Camera

Captures the road surface so the detection stage can identify a damaged region.

02Depth sensing

Adds geometric information such as the pothole's width, length and depth.

03GPS

Associates the detected pothole with a physical location for later reference.

04Navigation

Guides the vehicle toward the target and aligns it for the repair operation.

05Repair

The mechanical system dispenses and levels repair material, followed by a quality check.

03 / Sustainable engineering

THE R5 APPROACH.

The project applies five material-management principles to reduce waste and consider recovery wherever technically suitable.

REDUCEUse measured geometry to avoid excess material.
REUSEReuse suitable recovered material where appropriate.
RETRIEVERecover useful material from damaged sections when feasible.
REDESIGNMove from repeated manual inspection to targeted intervention.
RECYCLEKeep suitable waste within a material-recovery cycle.
Prototype status

IPRV V3.1 demonstrates the intended physical platform and integrated system direction. The technical dossier identifies further validation as necessary, including detection accuracy, depth error, GPS error, navigation error, repair quality, material consumption and repeatability under controlled and real-road conditions. These are validation targets, not claimed measured results.
